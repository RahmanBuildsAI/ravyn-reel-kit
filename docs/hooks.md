# Hooks: the first 3 seconds

Loaded by the `reel-script` skill during the hook tournament. Everything here is a template: swap
the blanks for the real story and keep it **true**. A hook that lies trains people to leave.

## The one mechanic

Every strong hook **opens a loop and withholds the payoff.** The brain dislikes an unfinished
pattern, so it stays to close it. Four levers do the work, and most good hooks stack two:

1. **Curiosity gap.** Name "this", "it" or "something" without saying what it is.
2. **Personal stakes.** A confession or a cost. Honesty raises the question "what happened?"
3. **This is you.** Direct address. The viewer becomes the subject.
4. **Value for a tiny cost.** A big payoff for a few seconds of attention.

**The tell of a weak hook:** it answers its own question in line 1. A strong line 1 makes the
viewer need line 2.

## The seven formulas

| Formula | Template |
|---|---|
| Imperative ban | "Stop paying for [X]. [Free thing] just dropped." |
| Just-happened shock | "[Big company] just killed [X]." |
| Secret or leak | "The tool [company] doesn't want you to find." |
| First-person proof | "I built [thing] with AI in [real time span]." |
| Underdog plus number | "A [student / solo founder / unlikely person] built [X] for [real amount]." |
| Era declaration | "The era of [X] is over." |
| Stat stacking | "[N]x cheaper. [N]x faster. Free." |

Every bracket that holds a number takes a real, sourced number, or the formula is not used.

## Templates by lever

Most templates are first person. For a news reel, keep the mechanic and swap "I" for the story's
subject or for "you". Use confession and turning-point templates only when there is a real
personal story behind them. No fake drama on a news reel.

### Confession
- "I almost didn't post this, but you should see it."
- "I've never said this out loud before."
- "I was doing [X] wrong for [real time span]."
- "The thing I was most embarrassed about is now why people pay me."
- "I believed [common advice] for years. Then I tested it."

### Curiosity gap
- "Nobody saw this coming."
- "Here's what actually happened, because the headline got it wrong."
- "This wasn't supposed to be possible yet."
- "Then something happened I couldn't ignore."
- "It looked like a normal update. It wasn't."
- "The day I stopped doing [X] was the day it got easier."

### Value for a tiny cost
- "This took me [real time span] to figure out. You get it in 40 seconds."
- "I wish someone had shown me this sooner."
- "One setting. That's the whole trick."
- "The best part of [tool] is the one nobody uses."

### Turning point
- "Everything changed in one afternoon."
- "One decision changed the next [N] years."
- "This is the moment [industry] changed."
- "I almost missed this."

### This is you
- "If you use [tool], you need to see this."
- "You're probably already using it, and it can [surprising thing]."
- "You might be making the same mistake I was."
- "This is for anyone still paying for [X]."

### Before and after
- "[Real time span] ago, I couldn't [X]."
- "I went from [A] to [B] because of one change."
- "This used to take a team. Now it takes [one thing]."

## Viewer first

**Sentence one is about what the viewer gets. The story is sentence two, as proof.** Never open on
a bare fact about a company. "Company X just made Y free" is only interesting to someone already
invested. "You can stop paying for Y" makes the viewer the subject in four words, and everything
after it becomes evidence.

The three-part shape:

1. **Benefit.** What they walk away with, viewer as the subject.
2. **Proof.** The news or the story, framed as evidence for the benefit.
3. **Loop.** One short true line that says there is more ("And that's just the first one.").

**Where it does not apply:** pure drama or warning reels, where the story itself is the hook and
the viewer's stake is the alarm. The test: does the reel promise a payoff (a list, a tool, a
resource, a giveaway)? Open on the viewer. Does it end in a warning? Open on the story.

## The spike word

A hook is performed, not just read. **Every hook needs one word to peak on inside the first 8
seconds.** Mark it in bold in the script and say in the delivery note how to hit it. A pattern that
works: a small lift where the promise lands, a big lift on the spike. A hook can pass every check
in the tournament and still feel flat if there is nowhere to put the energy.

## Retention devices (keep them to the end, not just stop them)

Stack one into the hook on any list, countdown or giveaway reel:

- **Best is last.** "Stay to the end, the last one is the wildest." Then reorder so the final item
  genuinely is the standout.
- **Stay for the specific payoff.** Promise a concrete thing that only arrives at the end: a
  number, a twist, the free resource.
- **Close the loop last.** Ask a question in the hook, answer it in the final beat.

Name the payoff out loud. A counter on screen is not enough. **Never promise a payoff the ending
does not deliver.** That trains people to bounce from every future reel.

The shortest form carries the promise inside the benefit: "Watch this and you'll save money on
every AI tool you pay for" names the payoff and the window in one sentence.

## Trend break (only for saturated topics)

When a topic is already everywhere, do not pretend to be first.

Shape: **[the loud claim]. That's what everyone is saying. And they're right. But there's a catch.**

Open on what the viewer has already heard, say out loud that everyone is saying it, **agree**, then
add the part they left out. It turns other creators' reach into your setup and moves you from
"the fourth person making this video" to "the person explaining all of them".

- Conceding is the load-bearing part. You complete the claim, you do not debunk a true one.
- Only available if you genuinely have the missing part. No catch, no hook.
- **Never on a fresh story.** Inside the 24 to 48 hour window, speed is the advantage and this
  device throws it away.

## Shared-memory hook (for audiences rooted in one place)

If `CLAUDE.md` says most viewers share a country, city, school system or community, one
tournament option is always a **shared memory**: a true, specific moment everyone in that
audience remembers, set against the new story so the gap between them does the work. The viewer
knows in second one that the reel is about them, without a label.

- The memory must be true and checkable, with a source in the script.
- Prefer memories that build pride over ones that mock the viewer.
- A grievance memory ("we always get it last") needs an ending that turns it into pride or action.
- It is one option, not a forced winner.
